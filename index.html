About Me

Autonomous robotics, perception, and human–machine teaming

I'm Ibrahim Aromoye, a PhD researcher in Autonomous Robotics at the University of Glasgow. My work sits at the intersection of robot perception, motion planning, and human–robot interaction, with a focus on building systems that hold up in real, unstructured environments, not just controlled lab conditions.

Robots are increasingly expected to operate alongside people, in warehouses, clinics, field sites, and homes. My research asks what it actually takes for that to work: how a robot perceives its environment reliably, how it plans and adapts in real time, and how it coordinates with the humans around it in ways that feel natural and earn trust. I'm particularly drawn to problems where the gap between simulation and reality matters most.

My technical background spans the full autonomous stack, from embedded systems and low-level control to computer vision, deep learning, and system integration. I've worked across the development cycle: from early prototyping and sensor fusion to functional demos on real hardware. Tools and frameworks I work with regularly include ROS2, PyTorch, PyBullet, and OpenCV, alongside experience with platforms like UAVs, mobile manipulators, and collaborative robot arms.

Outside core robotics, I hold remote-pilot certification from the Malaysian Civil Aviation Authority (MCAA), which underpins my work in UAV-enabled field robotics and informs how I think about safe, responsible aerial deployment in real-world conditions.

My longer-term interests are as much about the field as they are about the technology. I want to contribute to robotics R&D and education that is grounded in sustainability; in how we build, deploy, and teach these systems, and I care about keeping the field accessible, particularly for communities that are underrepresented in advanced engineering.

If you're working on problems at the edge of what robots can reliably do today, I'd like to hear from you.

Robotics Projects

Sustainable Development Goals — Robotics in Context

Informational videos are a powerful medium for making complex ideas accessible, and this project put that to the test. The video explores how robotics intersects with the UN's Sustainable Development Goals, examining where autonomous systems can meaningfully contribute to challenges in areas such as clean energy, sustainable industry, and responsible innovation.

The goal was straightforward: communicate a nuanced technical-policy connection clearly and compellingly to a general audience, without oversimplifying the real limitations of what robotics can and cannot solve today.

Serial Message Decoding

For this project, I developed a Python script to decode serialised message data from binary files, converting raw binary output into human-readable values. The work involved byte-wise data extraction and protocol-level interpretation, parsing structured fields including system identifiers and sensor measurements, with the final output formatted into CSV for downstream analysis.

It is a compact but precise piece of low-level engineering: the kind of unglamorous work that sits at the foundation of any reliable data pipeline in embedded and autonomous systems, where clean, interpretable sensor output is non-negotiable.

A* Path Planning for Autonomous Mobile Robot Navigation

This project implements the A* path planning algorithm within a graphical interface for an autonomous mobile robot navigating a grid-based environment. The system finds optimal paths in real time while respecting directional movement constraints, using a Euclidean distance heuristic to guide search efficiently around obstacles.

My contribution covered the full implementation, from the core A* logic and constraint handling to the GUI that makes the planning process visually interpretable. The goal was to build a clean, functional baseline for grid-based robot navigation that demonstrates the practical trade-offs between heuristic choice, path optimality, and computational efficiency.

LQR Controller for UAV Stabilisation

This project involved developing a Python-based feedback control algorithm to stabilise the position of an Unmanned Aerial System in simulation. Working collaboratively, the implementation centred on a Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) approach, formulating the cost function, tuning the state and input weighting matrices, and validating hover and waypoint-tracking performance in a PyBullet simulation environment.

My contribution focused on the control design and tuning process, iterating on the Q and R matrices to balance positional accuracy against control effort, and handling practical challenges such as velocity estimation noise and actuator saturation that arise when moving from theory to simulation.

Study of LiDAR Accuracy — Field Evaluation Video

This video documents an experimental study of LiDAR measurement accuracy and reliability across varying environmental conditions, produced in the context of the Leo Rover autonomous mobile robot platform. It examines how real-world factors affect distance measurement performance, a practical consideration that directly informed sensor integration decisions on the rover. The goal was to move beyond datasheet specifications and develop grounded intuition for how LiDAR behaves in the field, where lighting, surface material, and range all interact in ways that matter for autonomous navigation.

Also on OneDrive

Autonomous Physical Interactions in Constrained Environments

This project involved developing an autonomous robot system capable of detecting, navigating to, and physically manipulating objects in constrained environments. The platform integrated a Leo Rover mobile base with a MyCobot 280 Pi arm, RealSense camera, RPLidar, and Intel NUC, coordinated over a ROS2 Jazzy framework.

My contribution centred on the arm control node, implementing coordinate-based inverse kinematics with fixed-orientation constraints to ensure reliable, repeatable end-effector positioning during pick-and-place tasks. The goal was to bridge the perception and manipulation layers cleanly, so that object poses detected by the vision pipeline could be executed accurately by the arm in a real, physically constrained workspace.

Development and Simulation of a Hybrid LTA Drone for Autonomous Pipeline Monitoring

This project presents a hybrid drone system for autonomous oil and gas pipeline inspection, addressing the limitations of traditional and drone-based methods in endurance and human dependence. The proposed design integrates lighter-than-air (LTA) and heavier-than-air (HTA) components to enhance flight time and stability.

My contribution focused on the system modelling and simulation pipeline, translating the hybrid aerodynamic concept into a testable dynamic model and validating autonomous flight behaviours in simulation before any physical build.

The goal was to demonstrate that a buoyancy-assisted design could meaningfully extend operational range and reduce reliance on human operators, moving pipeline inspection closer to a fully autonomous, deploy-and-monitor workflow.

Comparative Analysis of YOLO Models on Welding Helmet Detection

Welding is an essential process in industries such as construction, oil and gas, and manufacturing, but it poses significant safety risks. This work presents an automated helmet detection system using computer vision techniques to enhance safety compliance.

My contribution centred on benchmarking multiple YOLO model variants, evaluating trade-offs in detection accuracy, inference speed, and robustness across real-world welding environments.

The goal was to identify the most practical architecture for deployment in safety-critical industrial settings, where reliable real-time detection of PPE compliance can directly reduce workplace injury.

P-DETR: A Transformer-based Algorithm for Pipeline Structure Detection

Pipelines are critical infrastructure for transporting oil and gas but are highly susceptible to defects. This project introduces Pipe Detection Transformer (P-DETR), a novel transformer-based deep learning model designed for autonomous pipeline detection and potential integration with aerial robotic systems.

My contribution involved adapting the DETR architecture to the specific challenges of pipeline structure detection, including handling elongated, low-contrast structures and varying environmental conditions.

The goal was to develop a detection backbone robust enough for real-world aerial deployment, bridging the gap between state-of-the-art vision transformers and the practical demands of autonomous infrastructure inspection.

Development of Smart Bidirectional Digital Counter with Light Controller System

With the growing demand for intelligent automation, this project was developed to modernise traditional methods of occupancy monitoring and energy management. The system automatically counts people entering or leaving and restricts access when maximum capacity is reached, with automated lighting control to reduce energy consumption.

My contribution spanned the full embedded design cycle, from circuit design and sensor integration to firmware logic for bidirectional counting and real-time lighting control.

The goal was to deliver a low-cost, deployable solution that replaces manual occupancy tracking with a reliable automated system, demonstrating how embedded intelligence can drive meaningful energy savings in everyday built environments.

Ultrasonic Radar for Obstacle Detection

This project was developed to explore the principles of object detection and distance measurement using ultrasonic sensing technology. The Ultrasonic Radar System detects obstacles and measures non-contact distances ranging from 2 cm to 40 cm, scanning across an angular range of 15° to 165°.

My contribution covered the hardware assembly and software integration, programming the servo-driven scanning mechanism, processing ultrasonic echo timings into real-time distance readings, and building a visual radar display to surface detections intuitively.

The goal was to build hands-on intuition for range sensing fundamentals that underpin more advanced robotic perception systems, from proximity avoidance to early-stage mapping.
